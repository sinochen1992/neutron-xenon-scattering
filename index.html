What the evaluated libraries say

A neutron that scatters once on a xenon nucleus gives it a recoil energy fixed by the scattering angle: forward scattering gives the smallest recoils and backward scattering the largest, up to about 74 keV at 2.45 MeV, the deuterium–deuterium (DD) neutron energy. The angular distribution is therefore the recoil spectrum. The top-left plot shows that recoil spectrum, with \((1-\cos\theta)/2\), the fraction of the largest recoil, along its top edge; the top-right plot shows the same scattering against the angle \(\theta\). ENDF/B-VII.1 (red) and JENDL-4.0u (green) give different distributions; JEFF-4.0 (gold) can be added. Drag the energy or choose an isotope, and the plots redraw at once.

The mathematics behind the plots

Angle, solid angle and recoil energy. Write \(\mu = \cos\theta\), with \(\theta\) the scattering angle in the centre-of-mass (CM) frame. Solid angle is \(d\Omega = 2\pi\,d\mu\), so equal steps in \(\mu\) are equal solid angle, and the elastic cross section is \(2\pi\) times the area under \(d\sigma/d\Omega\) against \(\mu\). One elastic scatter gives the nucleus the fraction \((1-\cos\theta)/2 = \sin^2(\theta/2)\) of the largest possible recoil energy; that fraction is the scale along the top of the top-left plot. The recoil spectrum is therefore the angular distribution against \(\mu\), read backwards. The momentum transfer \(q\) is linear in \(\theta\) at small angles, and its square is linear in \(\mu\).

\[ \sigma_{\rm el} = 2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\,d\mu \] \[ E_R = E_{\max}\,\frac{1-\mu}{2},\qquad E_{\max} = \frac{4\,m_n M}{(m_n+M)^2}\,E_n,\qquad \frac{d\sigma}{dE_R} = \frac{4\pi}{E_{\max}}\,\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \] \[ q = 2k\sin\frac{\theta}{2},\qquad q^2 = 2k^2(1-\mu),\qquad E_R = \frac{\hbar^2q^2}{2M} \]

Partial waves \(\ell\): the amplitude. The incoming wave is a sum of partial waves of orbital angular momentum \(\ell\). The nucleus multiplies the outgoing part of each by one complex number \(S_\ell = \eta_\ell\,e^{2i\delta_\ell}\), with phase shift \(\delta_\ell\) and \(0 \le \eta_\ell \le 1\) (\(\eta_\ell \lt 1\) means absorption). The shape-elastic differential cross section is \(|f(\theta)|^2\), written here without the neutron spin, whose spin–orbit part adds a spin-flip term that vanishes at \(0^\circ\). Waves with \(\ell\) well above \(kR\) (about 2.1 for xenon at 2.45 MeV) miss the nucleus; \(\ell = 3\) and 4 reach only its diffuse surface, so \(\ell = 0\)–4 scatter (the partial-wave plot).

\[ f(\theta) = \frac{1}{2ik}\sum_\ell (2\ell+1)\,(S_\ell-1)\,P_\ell(\mu),\qquad \Big(\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}\Big)_{\rm shape} = |f(\theta)|^2 \] \[ \sigma_{\rm shape} = \frac{\pi}{k^2}\sum_\ell (2\ell+1)\,|1-S_\ell|^2,\qquad \sigma_{\rm reaction} = \frac{\pi}{k^2}\sum_\ell (2\ell+1)\,\big(1-|S_\ell|^2\big) \]

Legendre orders \(L\): the cross section. Squaring a sum of Legendre polynomials \(P_\ell\) gives products \(P_\ell P_{\ell'}\), and each product is a sum of \(P_L\) with \(|\ell-\ell'| \le L \le \ell+\ell'\) and \(\ell+\ell'+L\) even. The cross section is therefore a Legendre series in \(\mu\) up to \(L = 2\ell_{\max}\), 8 for xenon at 2.45 MeV. This series is how the evaluated files store elastic scattering: the elastic cross section \(\sigma_{\rm el}\) and the coefficients \(a_L\), with \(a_0 = 1\). Each coefficient is the average of its polynomial over the distribution, so \(a_1\) is the mean of \(\cos\theta\).

\[ P_\ell(\mu)\,P_{\ell'}(\mu) = \sum_L \langle \ell\,0\,\ell'\,0\,|\,L\,0\rangle^2\,P_L(\mu) \] \[ \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\sigma_{\rm el}}{2\pi}\sum_L \frac{2L+1}{2}\,a_L\,P_L(\mu),\qquad a_L = \int_{-1}^{1}P_L(\mu)\,p(\mu)\,d\mu,\qquad p = \frac{2\pi}{\sigma_{\rm el}}\,\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \]

A Legendre order is not a partial wave. With only s and p waves (\(\ell = 0, 1\)) and no absorption, the cross section below has an \(L = 1\) term that comes from the interference of the s and p waves. A p wave alone gives \(\mu^2 = (1+2P_2)/3\), orders \(L = 0\) and 2. A \(\cos\theta\) term in a library, or a difference between libraries in \(L = 1\), is therefore not "the p wave": every order mixes several partial waves. Compound elastic, averaged over many compound states, is symmetric about \(90^\circ\) and has even \(L\) only.

\[ \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{1}{k^2}\Big[\sin^2\delta_0 + 6\sin\delta_0\sin\delta_1\cos(\delta_0-\delta_1)\,\mu + 9\sin^2\delta_1\,\mu^2\Big] \]

The forward limit. The optical theorem ties the total cross section to the imaginary part of the forward amplitude. Since \(|f(0)|^2 \ge (\operatorname{Im} f(0))^2\) and compound elastic only adds, every consistent description has a forward elastic cross section of at least \((k\sigma_{\rm tot}/4\pi)^2\). In a file, with \(P_L(1) = 1\), the forward value is the sum below. When the total, the elastic cross section and the \(a_L\) come from one calculation, the bound holds automatically; when they are fixed separately, it need not (next section).

\[ \sigma_{\rm tot} = \frac{4\pi}{k}\,\operatorname{Im} f(0),\qquad \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}(0) = \frac{\sigma_{\rm el}}{2\pi}\sum_L \frac{2L+1}{2}\,a_L \;\ge\; \Big(\frac{k\,\sigma_{\rm tot}}{4\pi}\Big)^2 \]

The black sphere. \(S_\ell = 0\) for every wave that reaches the nucleus (\(\ell \le \ell_c \approx kR\)) and 1 for the others. The amplitude below is then purely imaginary, so the forward limit holds with equality; the elastic and absorption cross sections are equal; and the real sum changes sign between its lobes, so the minima are exact zeros. A real nucleus refracts the wave: its real potential gives phase shifts, so \(S_\ell\) is complex, the amplitude has a real part, and its single minimum near \(94^\circ\) at 2.45 MeV is not an exact zero; compound elastic fills it further.

\[ f(\theta) = \frac{i}{2k}\sum_{\ell\le\ell_c}(2\ell+1)\,P_\ell(\mu),\qquad \sigma_{\rm shape} = \sigma_{\rm reaction} = \frac{\pi}{k^2}\,(\ell_c+1)^2 \]

Why the libraries differ

They are built differently. Each library's xenon files come from a different chain of calculations and measurements, read from the files' own descriptions. In JENDL-4.0u one calculation gives the total, the elastic cross section and the angular shape. In ENDF/B-VII.1's files for Xe-132, 134 and 136 (…% of natural xenon), taken from the Chinese evaluation CENDL-3, these three numbers are fixed separately.

Three files fail a physical bound. The forward limit of the previous section requires the forward elastic cross section to be at least \((k\sigma_{\rm tot}/4\pi)^2\), with \(k\) the centre-of-mass wave number. At 2.45 MeV the three CENDL-3 files of ENDF/B-VII.1 fall …% below it, so their total, elastic cross section and angular shape cannot come from one scattering amplitude. JENDL-4.0u and JEFF-4.0 satisfy the bound for every isotope. Passing does not prove a file right.

A model for comparison

The blue and orange curves come from a calculation, not from a library. Shape elastic is the scattering by the average neutron–nucleus potential (Koning–Delaroche, spherical). Compound elastic, where the neutron is absorbed into a compound nucleus that then re-emits it, comes from a Hauser–Feshbach calculation (TALYS) with the same potential and has its calculated angular shape. The slider moves only within that calculation's range with a width-fluctuation correction: at 2.45 MeV, … of the reaction cross section, and almost nothing above … MeV.

The model is one potential. It does not show which library is right, and the difference between a library and the model's shape elastic is not compound elastic: it depends on the potential chosen and can be negative. JEFF-4.0 is the same kind of calculation with the same potential, so agreement between the model and JEFF-4.0 is not an independent check.

Details

Reading the plots
  • Recoil (top left): the elastic events as xenon recoil energy, \(E_R = E_{\max}(1-\cos\theta)/2\) and \(d\sigma/dE_R = (4\pi/E_{\max})\,d\sigma/d\Omega\) for one isotope. The top scale gives \((1-\cos\theta)/2 = E_R/E_{\max}\): 0 for forward scattering, 1 for backward. It is exact for one isotope; natural xenon sums nine isotopes with slightly different endpoints, and the scale uses the mean-mass endpoint, which the vertical marker shows. Dashed red lines mark minima of the model curve.
  • Angle (top right): \(d\sigma/d\Omega\) is the cross section per unit solid angle, in b/sr (1 b = \(10^{-28}\) m²), against the neutron's centre-of-mass scattering angle \(\theta\). Near \(0^\circ\) and \(180^\circ\) a degree holds little solid angle (\(d\Omega = 2\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta\)), so this view enlarges the forward peak and the backward rise compared with their share of the recoils. Deep minima come from interference between partial waves.
  • ENDF minus JENDL: the signed difference in b/sr against \((1-\cos\theta)/2\), 0 forward and 1 backward as on the recoil plot's top scale, with \(\theta\) along the top, and the constant that has the same integrated area (dashed). Its readout gives the solid-angle RMS about that constant, a description, not an uncertainty.
  • Partial waves: each \(\ell\) is an orbital-angular-momentum component of the wave. Blue bars sum to the shape-elastic cross section, orange bars to the cross section removed from the elastic wave. Integrating over angle removes the interference, so the bars cannot show where the minima are; \(kR\) is a rough scale for how many waves matter.
  • Potential: the real depth and the absorptive depth (volume plus surface) against distance from the nuclear centre; \(R\) is the radius and the arrow the reduced wavelength \(1/k\). For natural xenon the panel draws the mean-mass potential, while scattering is calculated isotope by isotope.

The optional black-sphere and billiard-ball curves are idealizations for contrast: a sharp absorbing sphere, and classical hard spheres scattering isotropically.

Cross-section accounting
\[ \sigma_{\rm total} = \sigma_{\rm elastic} + \sigma_{\text{non-elastic}} \] \[ \sigma_{\rm elastic} = \sigma_{\rm shape} + \sigma_{\text{compound elastic}} \] \[ \sigma_{\rm reaction} = \sigma_{\text{compound elastic}} + \sigma_{\text{non-elastic}} \]

Elastic leaves the nucleus in its original state; non-elastic is everything else: inelastic scattering to an excited state, capture with gamma emission, and other open channels. These plots show elastic recoils only.

Shape and compound describe how an elastic outcome is reached, not further final states. The model removes the reaction cross section from the elastic wave; part of it returns as compound elastic, the rest is non-elastic, so compound elastic moves cross section between the two groups and the total stays fixed. For natural xenon at 2.45 MeV the spherical calculation gives … b of shape elastic and … b of reaction cross section. A calculation that also follows the excitation of low-lying levels, such as the OPTMAN panel below, splits off that direct excitation first; a compound fraction means nothing until its denominator is stated.

The evaluated files store total, elastic and angular data (ENDF reaction identifiers MT=1 and MT=2, files 3 and 4). Their Legendre coefficients \(a_L\) describe the angular distribution; they do not label shape or compound mechanisms.

Where each library's xenon files come from (five releases)
A coupled-channel calculation for Xe-132 (OPTMAN)

OPTMAN is an optical-model code that can couple the excitation of low-lying levels to elastic scattering. These saved calculations cover Xe-132 at 2.45 and 14.1 MeV, with the Kunieda coupled-channel potential and, as a cross-check, the spherical Koning–Delaroche potential. Compound elastic is taken from the TALYS calculation and removed from the remaining absorption, so the total stays fixed.

Filling the minimum versus matching the area

An isotropic addition that fills a library's diffraction minimum over the model's shape elastic is not the one that matches the library's elastic cross section. The plots show, against angle, the isotropic fraction of the reaction cross section that would make the model equal each library there (solid), the value that matches the area (dashed) and the value that fills the minimum (dotted). The local value varies strongly and turns negative where the library lies below the model, so no single isotropic addition reproduces either library; a negative value is not a negative compound cross section.

\[ f_{\rm area} = \frac{\sigma_{\text{library elastic}} - \sigma_{\rm shape}}{\sigma_{\rm reaction}} \] \[ f_{\rm local}(\theta) = \frac{4\pi\,\big[(d\sigma/d\Omega)_{\rm library} - (d\sigma/d\Omega)_{\rm shape}\big]}{\sigma_{\rm reaction}} \]

None of these numbers is a measured or evaluated compound fraction; they are residuals of one chosen model.

Coverage, precision and sources

The shared bundle contains ENDF/B-VII.1, the qualified JENDL-4.0u comparison and JEFF-4.0 for the nine stable xenon isotopes, the spherical-model grid from 0.01 to 20 MeV, the TALYS compound-elastic range and shape, and the saved OPTMAN examples. The evaluated grid includes exact 2.45 and 14.1 MeV samples and 1° angles. Energies inside resonance ranges that are not reconstructed are masked, and a mixture needs every isotope. A second ENDF reader, written separately, reproduces the stored evaluated curves at 2.45 MeV within float32 precision.

JENDL qualification: the Xe files are the NNDC JENDL-4.0 snapshot. JAEA's update register lists no Xe update, and their angular coefficients reproduce those of the IAEA JENDL-4.0u conversion for Geant4 to 10⁻¹⁶ (orders up to 10, 1–3 MeV); byte identity with the 4.0u archive has not been established.

The model grid interpolates in log energy and log angular cross section; the evaluated display interpolates linearly in energy and \(\cos\theta\). Deep minima between energy samples can have large relative errors: this is a teaching display, not a transport or fitting database. Non-relativistic kinematics use \(M = A \times\) amu, except the forward-limit test above, which uses the relativistic wave number.
